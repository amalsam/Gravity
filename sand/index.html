Falling Sand simulation

Free falling sand game in your browser. Paint with sand, water, wood, fire and stone: sand piles up, water flows, fire spreads through wood and boils water into steam — all from simple cellular automata rules.

What you’re looking at

A falling-sand sandbox built on cellular automata. The screen is a grid of cells; every frame each cell looks at its neighbours and follows a small rule for its material — sand falls and piles, water flows sideways, fire rises and burns out, stone stays put. Scanning the grid from the bottom up keeps falling material from moving twice in one frame.

How to use it

  1. 1The demo scene pours sand and water for a few seconds — then it's yours. Drag to paint with the selected material.
  2. 2Set fire to the wooden frame, or pour water on the flames to make steam.
  3. 3Change the brush size for broad strokes or fine detail; use Erase to dig channels.
  4. 4Load the demo scene again or clear the canvas to start over.

Questions

How does a falling sand game work?

+

The canvas is a grid where each cell holds a material. Every frame, each cell checks the cells around it and moves according to simple rules — sand drops down or diagonally, water also spreads sideways. Complex piles and flows emerge from those rules.

Why scan from the bottom up?

+

If the grid were updated top to bottom, a grain could fall into a cell that is processed again later in the same frame and drop several rows at once. Updating bottom-up moves each grain at most once per frame.
