Gravity Engine simulation

Free online gravity simulator. Drag to stream glowing particles toward a star and watch 2D gravity bend them into rosette orbits, rings and slingshots in real time.

What you’re looking at

A gravity simulator set in a two-dimensional universe. In 2D, gravity weakens as 1/r rather than the familiar 1/r², which has two striking consequences you can see on screen: every circular orbit has the same speed, whatever its size, and orbits that aren't circular precess into flower-like rosettes instead of closing into ellipses. Particles are coloured by speed relative to orbital speed, so you can watch them fall inward (blue), circle (white) or swing outward (amber).

How to use it

  1. 1Press and drag anywhere on the canvas to stream particles toward the star.
  2. 2Choose a brush — single, circle, horizontal line or vertical line — to change how particles spawn. The Orbit brush launches them at orbital speed so they stay in orbit.
  3. 3On a keyboard, hold 1, 2 or 3 for rapid-fire line, cluster and column patterns; Ctrl-click adds one particle.
  4. 4Use Clear to start over, or Seed a disk to add a ready-made orbiting disk.

Questions

Why does gravity follow 1/r in this simulation?

+

The simulation is set in a flat 2D world. Gravity's pull spreads around a circle instead of over a sphere, so it weakens as 1/r rather than 1/r². It is the honest 2D version of Newton's law.

Why do the orbits look like flowers?

+

Only the 1/r² and spring force laws produce orbits that close on themselves (Bertrand's theorem). Under 1/r gravity the closest point of a non-circular orbit advances every lap, tracing a rosette.

What do the colours mean?

+

Colour shows speed compared with circular-orbit speed: white is orbital, blue is slower (falling inward) and amber is faster (swinging outward).

Do I need to install anything?

+

No. The simulator runs entirely in your browser on desktop or mobile, with no download or sign-up.
