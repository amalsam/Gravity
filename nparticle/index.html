N-Particle Sandbox simulation

Interactive 3D N-body gravity simulation in your browser. Spawn particles or bursts, tune mass and time dilation, and watch clusters, binaries and orbits emerge from pairwise O(N²) forces.

What you’re looking at

A 3D N-body sandbox where every particle attracts every other particle. With N bodies that is N·(N−1)/2 pairwise force calculations per frame, which is why this kind of simulation is described as O(N²). A softening term keeps close encounters numerically stable, and over time the system clumps into clusters, binary pairs and orbiting groups.

How to use it

  1. 1Click or tap on the canvas to spawn a particle; switch to Burst to spawn a cluster.
  2. 2Drag to orbit the camera, right-drag or two-finger drag to pan, and scroll or pinch to zoom.
  3. 3Change particle size and mass, or slow time down with time dilation.

Questions

What is an N-body simulation?

+

A simulation of N objects that all attract each other gravitationally. There is no general closed-form solution for three or more bodies, so the motion is computed step by step numerically.

Why is it called O(N²)?

+

Each of the N particles computes the force from every other particle, so the work per frame grows with the square of the particle count. Doubling the particles roughly quadruples the work.

What does the softening parameter do?

+

It adds a small constant ε to the distance in the force law, F ∝ 1 / (r² + ε²), so two particles passing very close do not produce an infinite force and fling each other away.
