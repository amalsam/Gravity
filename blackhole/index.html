3D Black Hole simulation

Interactive 3D black hole simulation in WebGL. Orbit a glowing accretion disk of 10,000 particles and see gravitational lensing, Doppler beaming and matter crossing the event horizon.

What you’re looking at

A real-time 3D black hole rendered with Three.js and WebGL. Thousands of particles orbit in an accretion disk; those that cross the event horizon (the Schwarzschild radius) are swallowed. The renderer approximates gravitational lensing — light from behind the hole bent into an Einstein ring — and relativistic Doppler beaming, which makes the side of the disk moving toward you brighter.

How to use it

  1. 1Drag with one finger or the mouse to orbit the camera around the black hole.
  2. 2Scroll or pinch with two fingers to zoom in toward the event horizon.
  3. 3Watch the Eaten counter climb as particles cross the event horizon; each one respawns at the outer edge of the disk.

Questions

What is the Schwarzschild radius?

+

It is the radius of the event horizon of a non-rotating black hole, r_s = 2GM / c². Anything that crosses it — including light — cannot escape. In this simulation, particles that cross it are removed and counted as eaten.

Why is one side of the accretion disk brighter?

+

Relativistic Doppler beaming. Gas orbiting toward the viewer at a large fraction of light speed appears brighter and bluer; gas moving away appears dimmer and redder.

Is this an accurate black hole?

+

It is a real-time approximation for intuition and education. Lensing and beaming are modelled visually rather than by full general-relativistic ray tracing, so it runs smoothly in a browser.
